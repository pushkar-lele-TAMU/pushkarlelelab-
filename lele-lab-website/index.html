OUR RESEARCH

Mechanisms of infection & resistance

We deploy computational and experimental approaches to investigate how pathogens perceive and respond to environmental stresses.

Antibiotic tolerance

We study the mechanisms by which bacteria develop tolerance against beta-lactam antibiotics, focusing on epigenetic phenomena and short-time adaptations. Working at the single-cell level, we correlate adaptive responses with alterations in the mechanical properties of the bacterial cell wall.

Immune–pathogen interactions

The innate immune system is our first line of defense against infection. We investigate how neutrophils exhibit diverse responses when targeting pathogenic bacteria, combining microfluidics, optical trapping, and single-molecule imaging.

Spread of carcinogenic bacteria

Helicobacter pylori infections are a major cause of peptic ulcers and gastric cancers. We investigate how the chemotaxis signaling network modulates H. pylori's flagellar functions to promote colonization.

Cell mechanics & mechanosensing

Bacteria must sense attachment to a surface before establishing biofilms. We research mechanosensing in bacteria to determine how biofilms form, building on the discovery that flagellar protein components function as mechanosensors.
